# ADR-0008: An accent colour and status colours

- **Context**: the UI was strictly monochrome (the only hue was `danger`), and read as bland: nothing said "you are here" or "good" or "look at this". The user asked for some colour, not much.
- **Decision**: add three theme tokens, **`accent`**, **`success`** and **`warning`**, next to `danger`. Every theme defines them from its own palette (its blue, green and yellow; Rosé Pine's gaps are filled with close matches) and they are held to the same contrast rules as text: 4.5:1 on canvas and panel and 3.5:1 on the selected row, enforced by the theme tests, because they are used as text and as the fill behind canvas-coloured button labels.
- **Where colour appears (and nowhere else)**: `accent` for the current screen (nav bar and icon), page-header icon tiles, the open row's bar, primary buttons, focus rings, checkboxes, today (week strip, timeline line), the critical path and mentions; `success` for on-track / absorbed / done; `warning` for amber health, stale items and top-priority chips; `danger` unchanged (errors, overdue, destructive).
- **Rules kept**: flat (no shadows or gradients), theme tokens only (no raw colours), and **colour never carries meaning alone**: health marks keep their shapes, chips keep their words.
- **Consequences**: a theme is now 14 colours (`Tokens` grew three fields, `css_vars` returns 14, `input.css` holds the defaults); adding a theme means choosing three more values. A user-imported palette (a possible later feature) would need them too.
